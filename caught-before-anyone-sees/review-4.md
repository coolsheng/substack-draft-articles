**Thesis (lean):** What AI-assisted learning quietly destroys isn't speed or competence — it's the shared vocabulary teammates only build when they fail in public together.

**Proposed shape:**
1. The Friday-I-wiped-production scene — what the room actually learned was the *language* for the bug, not the fix.
2. AI lets us each learn the fix in private — fast, painless, and in nine different private vocabularies.
3. The thing being lost: at 2am next year, nine engineers debug the same bug as strangers. The answer to your own closing question — the "missing piece" is the shared reference, not the wisdom.
4. Claim ending — name the trade. Faster learners, worse teammates. That's the bill.

---

**Diagnosis:**

1. **Buried thesis** — the actual insight ("the thing being lost wasn't in the public execution. It was in the language we built around the lesson") arrives in section 3 of 4. The opening line "AI made us faster learners but worser teammates" gestures at it, but "worser teammates" is too vague to be the hook — *which* part of teamwork is broken? Lead with the language claim, then earn it with the Friday scene. This is also the direct answer to your closing question: the missing piece is the *shared reference*, not "wisdom" abstractly. You've written the answer; you just haven't promoted it to the top.

2. **Vibe ending (1A failure)** — the close is a three-question spiral: "destination or journey?" / "Neither." / "It's the company." / "What is the actual missing piece here?" The piece literally ends asking the reader to supply the answer. That's the textbook 1A violation — claim retreating into question. You already have the claim ("the language we built around the lesson"). Land on it flat. The reader shouldn't have to write the last sentence for you.

3. **Not salient** — the "Lab Grown Experts" section is about a *different* argument (can you be a real expert without walking the path?) than the shared-language thesis. It's adjacent, not load-bearing. Cutting it would tighten the spine and stop the piece from arguing two things at once. The "how do we know when we are experts" question is its own essay.

**Working:** "I wasn't just teaching them the fix, I was teaching them how to talk about the fix" is the spine sentence — that's the whole piece in one line. Promote it.
