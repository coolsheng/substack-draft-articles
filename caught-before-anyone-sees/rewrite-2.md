# Caught Before Anyone Sees

## What happens to learning when failure has no audience?

AI made us faster learners but worser teammates.

## The Friday I Wiped Production

Friday 2pm, circa 2019 BE (Before Claude) - I wiped the production database, again. 

Friday 5.30pm, we sweated a fix over drinks.

Monday 9am, I had to explain myself to a room of engineers why I am such a dumbass.

One man’s failure is a lesson for the other nine. 

The part I didn’t notice at the time - I wasn’t just teaching them the fix, I was teaching them how to talk about the fix. The wisdom and intuition of how we landed on the bug, the names and story - the shorthand that would, a year later still prove useful when any one of us debug the same issue at 2am.

This is the sort of communal event where tribal wisdom gets passed down, an experience that turns novice into experts.



“Witness me” - Production Bug

## Lab Grown Experts 

That’s the thought echoing through AI hustle culture - anyone can build anything, anyone can learn anything anyone can be anything.

With enough tokens, we get to create personalised masterclasses (“As a senior/veteran/expert X”) from the comfort of our own space; grow at our own pace and fail until we ace.

However, how do we know when we are experts when we haven’t truly walked a path of experience?



“Make no mistakes” - AI Agent

## If You Know, You Know

But we are learning faster AND we save ourselves from public embarrassment?

No, because the thing being lost wasn’t in the public execution. It was in the language we built around the lesson.

When everyone fills the gap privately, no two teammates carry the same reference back. Different prompts, different agents, different patched understanding of the same problem - the answer is correct, but is it right?



Intelligence is knowing fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.

## Standing on the Shoulder of Giants

Public failures is not the only path for wisdom but it was a communal catalyst for knowledge to be shared, in an increasingly individualised world, I fear that this lesson is being lost. The lesson was always the excuse - we are simply telling stories for the ones after us.



In the pursuit of learning, is it the destination or the journey that matters?

Neither.

It’s the company.
