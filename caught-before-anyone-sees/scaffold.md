**Working title:** Caught Before Anyone Sees

**Thesis in one line:** Personalised AI learning is quietly privatising the lessons that teams used to share — and without the old ritual of public failure, knowledge is growing while the wisdom that binds a team is thinning out.

---

## Everyone's learning, alone
- Failure is privatised — you fail to your chat window, not your team
- The knowledge gap gets filled, but by AI intelligence, not by anyone around you
- Each person's path through the same problem now looks different — different prompts, different framings, different patched understandings

## Failure used to be a team sport
- The old way: failure happened in the open — in front of a mentor, a team, a room
- Public failure was the catalyst that forced people to learn together, in structured ways
- The ritual wasn't the mistake — it was the story-sharing afterward, same shape, same beats, repeated until it became how a team thought together
- What got passed down wasn't the fix; it was wisdom, wrapped in a story

## Knowledge without a shared language
- Wisdom is communication-shaped; knowledge alone is not
- When everyone fills the gap privately, no two teammates carry the same reference back into the room
- AI gives you the answer; it doesn't give you the *shared* answer — and the team has nothing standardised left to ride on

## Learning was never just personal
- The individual curve is smoother — and lonelier
- The team-level curve is the one that's broken: the ritual that used to bind it is gone
- AI can transfer intelligence, but it can't perform the ritual — and teams solving hard problems need a shared story, not parallel private ones

---

**Alternative angles:**
- *Diagnostic / mechanism breakdown*: skip the personal framing and structure the piece around the specific mechanism — what ritualised public failure produced, what privatised learning replaces it with, and what falls out of the gap.
- *Reaction to the AI-learning consensus*: open against the prevailing take that personalised AI tutoring is unambiguously good, and use the team-communication cost as the under-discussed counter.
