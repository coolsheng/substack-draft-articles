# Caught Before Anyone Sees
### What happens to learning when failure has no audience?

Think back to 2019 BC (Before Claude). I was a dashing young developer. I made a huge mistake — wiped a production database on a Friday afternoon. We fixed it that night. We debriefed it Monday as a production incident. And most importantly, I learned from my public and catastrophic error.

I'm sure many engineers have formative stories like this. Back then, failure was performative and expected.

One engineer's failure was a public lesson for the other nine — tribal wisdom passed on through someone else's experience.

In 2026, I don't think this happens anymore. Failure is now a privatised experience, and knowledge doesn't get distilled from a communal ritual.

> "Witness me" — Production Bug

In modern software development, AI is synonymous with efficiency. The ideal engineer is bootstrapped into their 10x version. It's not just a marketing term — it's reality. Everyone can fail in the comfort of their own home at 10x the speed, getting a personalised learning experience through an LLM. The hyperbolic-learning chamber accelerates everything. You can become an expert at the speed of thought.

As long as you have enough tokens, humans need not make mistakes. Only your wallet is needed to find failure preemptively.

> "Make no mistakes" — AI Agent

The counter is that privatised failure, in the right environment, becomes a tight growth loop. Mistakes get identified immediately, feedback is thorough, and you fail in your own space at your own pace.

The issue — how do you know what to ask when you're learning something for the first time?

The experience is personalised but it doesn't have holistic depth. A persona prompt of "critique this from the perspective of a senior X" does not replace the wisdom of someone who has walked the path before.

*Intelligence is knowing that fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.*

I fear we are no longer learning together. It's a silent transfer — knowledge and skills can be learnt individually, but the gap can no longer be bridged by shared experience.

## Closing Thoughts

In the pursuit of learning, much like life — is it the destination or the journey that matters?

In my opinion, it's the company.

## Pass 2 — Bolder rewrite

# Caught Before Anyone Sees

Friday afternoon, 2019. I wiped a production database. We fixed it that night. Monday, we debriefed it as an incident — me at the front, nine other engineers in the room, the whole catastrophic thing laid out.

I learned. They learned too.

That kind of formative story used to be standard issue for software engineers. Failure was performative and expected. One engineer's mistake was a public lesson for the other nine — tribal wisdom transferred through someone else's wreckage.

I don't think that happens anymore.

In 2026, failure is private. AI is synonymous with efficiency — every engineer bootstrapped into a 10x version of themselves, failing in the comfort of their own home at 10x the speed, getting a personalised post-mortem from an LLM.

As long as you have enough tokens, humans need not make mistakes. Only your wallet is needed to find failure preemptively.

> "But surely that's better?"

The privatised version has its case. A tight feedback loop. Mistakes caught immediately. You fail in your own space, at your own pace, without an audience to apologise to.

But — how do you know what to ask when you're learning something for the first time?

*Intelligence is knowing fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.*

A persona prompt — "critique this from the perspective of a senior engineer" — does not replace someone who has actually walked the path. The personalised loop has range but not depth. It can tell you fire is hot. It cannot pass on the instinct.

What I miss isn't the failure. I miss the room. The nine other engineers who learned something they didn't have to learn the hard way. The silent transfer that happened when one person stood up and said *I broke it*.

Knowledge still moves. But the gap between us can no longer be bridged by shared experience.

In the pursuit of learning, much like life — is it the destination or the journey?

It's the company.
