## Pass 1 — Faithful tighten

# Caught Before Anyone Sees

Friday afternoon, 2019. I wiped a production database. We fixed it that night. Monday, we debriefed it as an incident — me at the front, nine other engineers in the room, the whole catastrophic thing laid out.

I learned. They learned too. One engineer's wreckage became tribal wisdom — performative, expected, transferred through someone else's mistake.

I don't think that happens anymore.

In 2026, failure is private. AI is synonymous with efficiency — every engineer bootstrapped into a 10x version of themselves, failing in the comfort of their own home at 10x the speed, getting a personalised post-mortem from an LLM.

As long as you have enough tokens, humans need not make mistakes. Only your wallet is needed to find failure preemptively.

> "But surely that's better?"

The privatised version has its case. A tight feedback loop. Mistakes caught immediately. You fail in your own space, at your own pace.

But — how do you know what to ask when you're learning something for the first time?

*Intelligence is knowing that fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.*

A persona prompt — "critique this from the perspective of a senior engineer" — does not replace someone who has walked the path. The personalised loop has range but not depth. It can tell you fire is hot. It cannot pass on the instinct.

And here is the part the efficiency story keeps quiet about — AI closes the knowledge gap. It does not close the *communication gap*.

When the senior engineer walked you through the wreckage, you weren't just absorbing the fix. You were absorbing the *language* of the fix. The names they gave the bug. The story they told about it. The shorthand that would, a year later, let your team debug a 2am outage by speaking a sentence and a half to each other.

AI gives you the fix. It does not give you the sentence and a half.

Each person on the team is now being tutored privately, in slightly different phrasings, against slightly different gaps. Everyone gets smarter. Everyone converges less. The knowledge stack gets taller. The communication stack gets thinner.

What I miss isn't the failure. I miss the room. The nine other engineers who learned something they didn't have to learn the hard way. The silent transfer that happened when one person stood up and said *I broke it*.

In the pursuit of learning, much like life — is it the destination, or the journey?

It's the company.

## Pass 2 — Bolder rewrite

# Caught Before Anyone Sees

AI closes the knowledge gap. It does not close the gap between us.

That's the thing I keep coming back to. Every engineer on the team is now being tutored privately, in slightly different phrasings, against slightly different gaps. Everyone gets smarter. Everyone converges less.

The knowledge stack gets taller. The communication stack gets thinner.

> "Isn't that just nostalgia?"

Friday afternoon, 2019 — I wiped a production database. We fixed it that night. Monday, we debriefed it as an incident, me at the front, nine other engineers in the room.

I learned. They learned too. One engineer's wreckage became tribal wisdom — performative, expected, transferred through someone else's mistake.

The part I didn't notice at the time — I wasn't just teaching them the fix. I was teaching them how to *talk* about the fix. The names we gave the bug. The story we told about it. The shorthand that would, a year later, let any one of us debug a 2am outage by speaking a sentence and a half to each other.

That shorthand is what private learning quietly skips.

*Intelligence is knowing fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.*

AI can tell you fire is hot. It cannot pass on the instinct. It also cannot give your team a shared vocabulary, a shared scar, or a moment to point back to when something breaks again.

So the bug gets fixed. The engineer gets smarter. The team gets quieter.

What I miss isn't the failure. I miss the room.

It's the company.
