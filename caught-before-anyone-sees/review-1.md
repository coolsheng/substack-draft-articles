## Pass 1 — Faithful tighten

# Caught Before Anyone Sees

> We can become experts overnight.

That's the thought echoing through AI hustle culture — anyone can build anything, anyone can learn anything, anyone can *be* anything.

The growth case writes itself:

- Personalised, persona-based masterclasses ("you are a senior X") from the comfort of our own space
- Knowledge gaps filled at our own pace
- Private failures, in private, so when we emerge we ace it

Privatised learning, privatised failing. We get to skip the audience.

*Intelligence is knowing fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.*

The intelligence stack gets taller. The wisdom stack gets thinner.

## The Friday I wiped production

Friday afternoon, 2019 — I wiped a production database. We fixed it that night. Monday, we debriefed it as an incident: me at the front, nine other engineers in the room.

I learned. They learned too. One engineer's wreckage became tribal wisdom — performed, expected, transferred through someone else's mistake.

The part I didn't notice at the time — I wasn't just teaching them the fix. I was teaching them how to *talk* about the fix. The names we gave the bug. The story we told about it. The shorthand that would, a year later, let any one of us debug a 2am outage by speaking a sentence and a half to each other.

That shorthand is what private learning quietly skips.

## Knowledge without a shared language

Wisdom is communication-shaped. Knowledge alone is not.

When everyone fills the gap privately, no two teammates carry the same reference back into the room. AI gives you the answer — it doesn't give you the *shared* answer. The team has nothing standardised left to ride on.

> "But the individual learns faster, doesn't that net out?"

The individual curve is smoother. And lonelier. The team-level curve is the one that's broken — the ritual that used to bind it is gone.

## Standing on the shoulder of giants

Learning in private was never the point. The lesson was always the excuse — what we were really doing was leaving a trail of stories for whoever came next.

> In the pursuit of knowledge, what's more important? The destination or the journey?

Neither.

It's the company.

## Pass 2 — Bolder rewrite

# Caught Before Anyone Sees

Friday afternoon, 2019 — I wiped a production database.

We fixed it that night. Monday, nine engineers sat in a room while I walked them through what I'd done. I was the wreckage; they were the audience.

What I didn't notice at the time was that I wasn't teaching them the fix. I was teaching them how to *talk* about the fix. The names we gave the bug. The story we agreed on. The shorthand that would, a year later, let any one of us debug a 2am outage in a sentence and a half.

*Intelligence is knowing fire is hot. Wisdom is knowing not to touch it.*

Wisdom is the shorthand. Knowledge is the fix. They are not the same thing.

## The privatised classroom

AI hustle culture sells a quieter version of the same outcome: anyone can learn anything, at their own pace, from a persona-tuned tutor, in the comfort of their own room. The failure is private. The recovery is private. By the time we emerge, we ace it.

Nothing about that sounds wrong. The individual curve is smoother. The hours-to-competence drops. The shame tax disappears.

But the team curve — the one that used to bend through someone else's bad Friday — that curve is flattening for a different reason. There is no longer a room.

> "So what? The individual learns faster, doesn't that net out?"

No, because the thing being lost wasn't in the lesson. It was in the language we built around the lesson.

When everyone fills the gap privately, no two teammates carry the same reference back. Different prompts, different framings, different patched understandings of the same problem. AI gives each of us an answer. It doesn't give us the *shared* answer. There's nothing standardised left for the team to ride on at 2am.

## The trail we used to leave

The lesson was always the excuse. What we were really doing was leaving a trail of stories for whoever came next.

> In the pursuit of knowledge, what's more important? The destination or the journey?

Neither.

It's the company.
