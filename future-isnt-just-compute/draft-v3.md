# Future Isn't Just Compute
> It's actually FDEs

Have a look this bombshell - https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership - released just as the US delegation was in China for their historic visit.
If you read past the geopolitics, Anthropic just gave us the cookbook of AI domination in their Four Fronts of Competition: Compute, Domestic Adoption, Global Distribution and Resilience as the pillars.

The most straightforward is compute.
The assumption is that compute is king and when you control it, the rest will fall in line.
That's the thesis at least, and it seems like it's working - compute is expected to gap against CCP's non-democratic AI at an exponential rate.
This kind of policy-driven effort does help - such as China's AI+ Initiative vs Trump's AI Action Plan, however, it's also the most diminishing as it understimates the complexity and most unpredicatble system of all - the people.
People are not easily
Let's look at how these AI labs are truly tackling these 3:

Remember when the term Cloud Migration was HOT? Yeah now it's same but it's called AI Native strategy.
The cloud architects? Reskinned to be called Forward Deployed Engineers.
This is their solution to tackling domestic migration - and it works, much like vendor lock-in with cloud, when your business flow or product has AI capabilities, ripping it out or migrating it is HARD. What do you replace it with? Humans?
No, once it's in, it's there to day, even to change AI models might not be as easy due to different models excelling at different things - you'll be risking your product experience.
Compute does not touch this - adoption does not come from intelligence of the models, it comes from Anthropic and OpenAI investing in hiring FDEs, Certification Designers and DevRelations - it's another ecosystem of GCP/AWS Certifications where new engineers define value in this AI-lead world.
This is the sort of democracy-lead moat that Chinese or OpenSource AI can't touch - you can't certify opensource for quality and Chinese AI while cheap, has yet to catch up on this space (compare the job listings)

Along a similar vein, FDEs are the cultural link between American AI and global AI distribution.
It's called - we throw a lot of money at local people and they help us implement an AI strategy into your business.
This works - it's a win-win and the only losers are the AI lab who came too late.
And if we look at job listing comparisons - I would say the Americans who has more money to throw are winning.

This sort of presence does one thing every policy hopes to do - attract talent. This is the biggest piece of the puzzle that fulfills the perfect loop of innovation where compute and talent multiplies against each other, breeding more innovation.
The Resilience pillar for a stable environment is just the cherry on top to ensure that the talent stays.


I would be a fool to say I am not enticed - this is the sort of once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for every engineer worth their salt. It's why FDEs are booming right now, it's why people are upskilling but most importantly, it's pushing engineers to actually have a product mindset and talk to people.
Do I find FDE roles interesting? Sure.
Do I like that this space is getting more competitive with an AI twist? 100%.
I am going to apply? Yeh.
If viewed from a political aspect, your job is genuinely important and is playing a role in shaping the AI economy at a global level, one job at a time.
So for someone looking for meaning in their work, as weird as it might sound, FDE might actually be the right choice.
