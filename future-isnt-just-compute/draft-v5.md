# The Future isn't just compute
> It's actually FDEs

Been wondering why forward deployed engineers (FDEs) are hot commodity for AI labs right now?
<Screenshot of AI lab job postings>
The answer lies in Anthropic's latest bombshell article - https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership - released just as the US delegation was in China for their historic visit.
If you read past the geopolitics, Anthropic just gave us the cookbook in AI domination in their Four Fronts of Competition: Compute, Domestic Adoption, Global Distribution and Resilience as the pillars.


## Compute is King
In the proverbial race to powerful AIs, compute power is presented as the main pillar for AI dominance - it's the technical, hard floor which transforms useless data into coherent, useful models. This compute advantage is why "democractic-lead" AIs are currently the frontier models which sets the benchmark for all others.
It's also why Chinese AI models are falling behind American frontier models - their lack of access to advanced AI chips (stupid, sexy NVDIA) is forcing them to play catch up in non-traditional ways (i.e. distillation attacks)
In another words, export control policies and government-lead restrictions have been very effective, they act as the ceiling which caps independent progress. (link Dario's Deepseek article)
Thus, whoever controls the ~spice~ compute, gets to dictate the rules of engagement. 
Although sounds omninous, I am not entirely against it, as there's safety via lead time where the advantaged side could push for more safety controls - something lead AI labs have been pushing, such as Anthropic's AI ethics (https://www.anthropic.com/constitution) - rather than a close competition where both sides would likely disregard safety for speed of innovation.


A king still needs its knights so while Anthropic presents compute as its central pillar, they also understand that intelligence and capability will not manifest into automatic adoption.
While policy-driven efforts does help with adoption - such as China's AI+ Initiative vs Trump's AI Action Plan, it's impact does not model the complexity and most unpredicatble system of all, the people.
So AI labs has to cook their own recipie in another way.

## New Kid in Town
Remember when the term cloud migration was HOT? 
Step aside cloud boomers, there's a new kid in town; it's the same kid as before but they're called AI transformation.
The harbringers of this? Forward Deployed Engineers

This is their solution to tackling domestic adoption - and it works, much like vendor lock-in with cloud, once it's in, it's here to stay.
Speed of business AI transformation is unprecedented and compute does not touch this - adoption does not come from intelligence of the models, it comes from Anthropic and OpenAI investing in hiring FDEs, Certification Designers and DevRelations - it's another ecosystem of GCP/AWS Certifications where new engineers define value in this AI-lead world.
This is the sort of democracy-lead moat that Chinese or OpenSource AI can't touch - you can't certify opensource for quality and Chinese AI while cheap, has yet to catch up on this space (compare the job listings)

Along a similar vein, FDEs are the cultural link between American AI and global AI distribution.
It's called: we throw a lot of money at local people and they help us implement an AI strategy into your business.
This works - it's a win-win and the only losers are the AI lab who came too late.

This sort of presence does one thing every policy hopes to do - attract talent. This is the biggest piece of the puzzle that fulfills the perfect loop of innovation where compute and talent multiplies against each other, breeding more innovation.
The Resilience pillar for a stable environment is just the cherry on top to ensure the talent stays.

## The Vicious Circle of AI
In a harmonious cycle where all of these fronts are won, what does that look like?
* Compute advantage creates better models -> attracts talent for adoption -> adoption spreads globally -> talent stays due to realised advantage -> leads to more innovation -> more compute advantage. (diagram this up)

It's a positive feedback loop, continually widening the gap between competitors, rewarding every compute-advanged country.
It's why compute export controls are so important.
It's why hiring FDEs are so important.
The combination of export control and relentless talent acquisition is the modern day battleplan to win this AI Cold War.
However, for every non-compute countries out there?
It'll be a brain drain of unprecedented proportion.

What does this mean for young dashing engineers like me?
The mechanics of AI adoption means that FDEs will play a pivotal role in this AI race - it's a career move that carries an unseen weight.
So the question I urge people to ask when they apply for FDE roles isn't "is this the best AI lab?" but "is this the AI lab I want to win?"
Because that's the question I will lead with for my applications.
