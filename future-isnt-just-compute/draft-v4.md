# Future isn't just compute
> It's actually the forward deployed engineers

Been wondering why forward deployed engineers (FDEs) are hot commodity for AI labs right now?
<Screenshot of AI lab job postings>
The answer lies in Anthropic's latest bombshell article - https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership - released just as the US delegation was in China for their historic visit.
If you read past the geopolitics, Anthropic just gave us the cookbook of AI domination in their Four Fronts of Competition: Compute, Domestic Adoption, Global Distribution and Resilience as the pillars.

But not all pillars are built equal and compute is the central pillar headlines and policies tend to focus on.
The assumption is that compute will be the floor which feed the other pillar downstream from an intelligence and capability perspective - if it's uesful, people will use it.
This logic diminishes the importance of the other pillars however, as it simplifies the complexity and most unpredicatble system of all - the people.
While policy-driven effort does help with adoption - such as China's AI+ Initiative vs Trump's AI Action Plan, this is the area where AI labs have to cook their own receipe:

> "Name's Engineer, Forward Deployed Engineer" - Employee 007

Remember when the term cloud migration was HOT? Step aside cloud boomers, there's a new kid in town; it's the same kid as before but they're called AI transformation.
The harbringers of this? Forward Deployed Engineers

This is their solution to tackling domestic adoption - and it works, much like vendor lock-in with cloud, once it's in, it's here to stay.
Speed of business AI transformation is unprecedented and compute does not touch this - adoption does not come from intelligence of the models, it comes from Anthropic and OpenAI investing in hiring FDEs, Certification Designers and DevRelations - it's another ecosystem of GCP/AWS Certifications where new engineers define value in this AI-lead world.
This is the sort of democracy-lead moat that Chinese or OpenSource AI can't touch - you can't certify opensource for quality and Chinese AI while cheap, has yet to catch up on this space (compare the job listings)

Along a similar vein, FDEs are the cultural link between American AI and global AI distribution.
It's called - we throw a lot of money at local people and they help us implement an AI strategy into your business.
This works - it's a win-win and the only losers are the AI lab who came too late.

This sort of presence does one thing every policy hopes to do - attract talent. This is the biggest piece of the puzzle that fulfills the perfect loop of innovation where compute and talent multiplies against each other, breeding more innovation.
The Resilience pillar for a stable environment is just the cherry on top to ensure that the talent stays.


I would be a fool to say I am not enticed - this is the sort of once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for every engineer worth their salt.
It's why FDEs are booming right now, it's why people are upskilling but most importantly, it's pushing engineers to actually have a product mindset and talk to people.
If viewed from a political aspect, your job is genuinely important and is playing a role in shaping the AI economy at a global level, one company AI transformed at a time.

So should we apply if we're excited about this space? 
I believe yes, because I know I'll be.
